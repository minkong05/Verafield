# **1. Executive Summary**

Starting 30 December 2026, non-compliant palm oil cannot be placed on the EU market unless the seller can prove exactly which plot of land it came from, and that the land was not deforested after 2020. Malaysia does not qualify for any shortcut process, so the EU operator carries the filing duty and flows the evidence requirement down by contract to every mill for every farm in its supply chain.

Malaysia already has good systems for tracking land coordinates and transactions (SIMS, GeoSAWIT, e-MSPO). What is missing is proof of land ownership, farm worker welfare records, and the paperwork that groups everything into one compliant “batch.” Small mills and farmers do not have the time, money or know-how to produce this themselves.

TAPAK is a field service that visits farms, collects the missing documents and evidence, and packages everything into the exact format EU buyers require. The mill pays for the service. The farmer pays nothing — this is allowed under EU rules, and it is also what makes mills trust and adopt the service quickly.

## **1.1 Why This Works**

* The deadline is fixed by law — mills have no choice but to comply.
* The mill pays, not the farmer, because it is the mill that loses the export order if evidence is missing.
* The work has to be repeated every year by law, so revenue is recurring, not one-off.
* Helping smallholders for free is not charity — it is what makes mills willing to sign up in the first place.

# **2. Problem Diagnosis & Market Fit**

## **2.1 The Regulatory Bottleneck**

The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) requires any company selling palm oil into the EU to prove, for every single plot of land involved, that the product is legal and deforestation-free. Malaysia is rated “standard risk” by the EU (not “low risk”), so mills here get no simplified process — they must submit full evidence for every farm.

There are six main pieces of information a mill must provide per shipment. Malaysia's national systems already cover the basic, easy parts. The hard, missing parts are the ones that actually require someone to visit the farm.

| **What's Needed** | **Already Covered by National Systems?** | **What TAPAK Provides** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product details, quantity, origin | Yes | Formatting and mapping only |
| Exact farm GPS location | Partly | Correct format, missing dates filled in |
| Proof of land ownership / right to farm | No | Collect land titles, tenancy agreements, etc. |
| Proof the land was not deforested since 2020 | No | Satellite comparison + planting history |
| Labour and consent records | No | On-site interviews and signed declarations |
| Full documentation pack per shipment | No | Assembled and delivered ready to submit |

## **2.2 Why This Matters Beyond Compliance**

In Sabah and Sarawak, many smallholders do not hold formal land titles — land is often passed down informally or held under customary rights. Proving legal use of the land is genuinely difficult here, more so than in Peninsular Malaysia. This also means labour conditions and community consent are poorly documented today. By collecting this evidence, TAPAK is not only solving an export problem — it is creating Malaysia's first structured record of labour and land-rights information for this group of farmers, which currently does not exist anywhere.

## **2.4 Cost of Inaction**

A typical smallholder farm produces goods worth roughly RM 8,200 a year in EU market “premium” value, but that value is lost entirely if the farm cannot prove compliance. Our fee is only about 5.5% of that value, so paying for the service is a clear win for the farmer and the mill. On the mill's side, just one undocumented farm can disqualify an entire truckload of fruit. For a mid-sized mill, that can mean losing over RM 1.5 million worth of eligible product in a single month.

# **4. Product Architecture**

We use technology where it helps, but our real product is verified evidence, collected and organised by trained field staff. We never rebuild what the government already provides, we plug the gaps.

## **4.1 What We Deliver**

| **Service** | **What the Mill / Farmer Gets** |
| --- | --- |
| Gap Assessment | A simple report showing what documents are missing for each farm |
| Farm Mapping | Accurate GPS boundary map of each plot |
| Land & Ownership Check | Collected and verified land title, lease, and inheritance documents |
| Labour & Rights Declaration | Signed labour arrangement, no-child-labour, and land-dispute declarations from each household |
| Deforestation Check | Satellite comparison proving no forest loss since 2020 |
| Evidence Pack | One ready-to-submit compliance file per shipment |
| Yearly Renewal | Re-checking and updating records, as required by law every year |

## **4.2 How It Works and Who Does the Work**

* Field staff visit farms with a mobile app to record GPS boundaries, photos, and signed consent forms — works offline in areas with poor signal.
* Months 1–6: A contracted GIS specialist manually reviews every satellite comparison alongside the system's output. Disagreements are used to calibrate verification thresholds, with the first 400 households serving as the training and calibration dataset.
* After calibration: As human–system agreement stabilises, the system handles routine cases while the specialist reviews only uncertain ones. By Month 6, most cases are processed automatically, the specialist's contract ends, oversight transfers to the CTO, and a ready-to-submit EU compliance pack is generated for the mill.

## **4.3 Legacy System Integration**

Where national systems already reach — SIMS, GeoSAWIT, e-MSPO — we simply consume their data through a read-only interface, keyed on the mill's MPOB licence number. Where they do not reach (land tenure, labour records, batch assembly), we fill the gap ourselves. If national coverage improves, our own collection cost falls and our margin rises — the parts we are paid for do not disappear. We do not touch the EU's own Article 33 submission system at all — that stays with the EU importer, not us.

## **4.4 What Is Actually Proprietary**

Our advantage is not the app — apps can be copied quickly. Three things are genuinely hard to copy, and each maps to a real evaluation point on technical defensibility.

| **Asset** | **What It Is** | **Why It's Hard to Copy** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State Land-Rights Rulebook | A guide mapping each state's land documents (title, lease, tenancy, native customary rights) to exactly what EUDR requires — different in every state | Not code — knowledge scattered across land offices and local custom. A competitor entering East Malaysia must rebuild this from zero |
| Five-Signal Data Check | Cross-checks GPS check-in, photo location, land area, MPOB licence records and yield against each other at the moment of collection; mismatches trigger review | The rules are easy to copy, but the trigger thresholds are tuned on real field data — our first 400 households are the training set a new entrant does not have |
| Auto-Assembled Buyer Pack | Turns raw household records into one ready-to-submit file per shipment, plus a live cleared / pending / frozen supplier list | No competitor in this specific market currently builds this per-batch assembly step — it is what turns data into a deliverable |

## **4.5 System Architecture**

| **Layer** | **What It Does** | **Technology (indicative)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collection | Offline mobile app for GPS, photos and signatures — works without signal, syncs later | React Native + local storage, background sync |
| Verification | Runs the Five-Signal Check; flags mismatches for a human to review | Rule engine + satellite imagery comparison, on managed cloud infrastructure with automated backups and horizontal scaling |

## **4.7 Who Builds What**

* Built in-house: the state rulebook and the verification thresholds — this is our core IP, and ownership is never outsourced. Initial calibration in the first six months is performed by a contracted GIS specialist under an IP-assignment agreement; oversight and rulebook maintenance transfer in-house to our CTO from month six.
* Outsourced: general app development, fixed-price contract (~RM 25,000, IP assigned to us in the contract); GIS/geospatial calibration, 6-month fixed-term contract (~RM 18,000, IP assigned to us in the contract).
* Referral only: land surveyors and lawyers — we refer and earn a fee; we do not perform statutory surveys or give legal advice ourselves.
* Local hires: field staff recruited directly from the Sabah/Sarawak districts we serve, given a two-week training period.

**4.8 Data Protection**

* Consent is collected under Malaysia's PDPA, and our data handling is designed to meet GDPR standards as well, since the evidence we produce ultimately reaches EU-based buyers.
* Only the last four digits of a farmer's MyKad are stored — full ID scans are never required by EUDR and never collected.
* Records are kept for five years, matching the retention period our mill clients' EU buyers are required to maintain under EUDR requirements — then deleted.
* Each mill can see only its own suppliers' data — no cross-mill visibility.

| GIS or legal outsourcing costs run higher than quoted | Both are quoted as ranges from real providers; we budgeted at the upper-middle of each range to leave room for variation |

# **Appendix A — EUDR Workload Classification & Field Cost Build-Up**

Not every piece of evidence EUDR asks for needs a person on the ground. Some can be pulled from a desk, from registries or systems that already exist. Others can only be collected by physically visiting the farm. This split is what decides our headcount — and therefore our biggest cost line.

| **Collection Mode** | **Share of the 92 Requirements** | **Example Items** | **Who Does It** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Desk — already held or easily generated | 26% | Product codes, net mass, HS codes, retention records | Compliance analyst, from HQ |
| Desk — pulled from a register or system | 19% | SSM trade name, 2020 land cover, MSPO certificate | Compliance analyst, from HQ |
| Field — must visit the farm in person | 45% | Land title, tenancy proof, labour declarations, FPIC records, GPS boundary | Field officer, on-site |
| Hybrid — field capture, then desk check | 11% | Area vs title variance, forest-loss threshold test | Field officer + analyst |

Just under half of everything EUDR requires has to be collected in person. That is why TAPAK is staffed as a field service business, not a software company, and why our field team is the **largest direct-delivery cost** in the Year 1 budget (Section 5.2).

# **Appendix B — Assumptions Register & Sensitivity**

Every number in this plan that is not a measured fact is an assumption. Here are the ones our model leans on most, and how we plan to test each one during the pilot.

| **Assumption** | **Value Used** | **How We'll Validate It** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FFB yield per hectare | 14 t/ha | Delivery records from the first 400 households |
| Average smallholder holding size | 3.8 ha | Farm boundary maps from Farm Mapping |
| Exchange rate | RM 5.00 = EUR 1; RM 4.20 = USD 1 | Restated at each reporting date |
| EU compliance premium | USD 150–220/t (mid-point USD 185) | Buyer contracts obtained via mill interviews |
| Field officer capacity | Up to 40 households/month | Measured monthly once the pilot starts |
| Reachable mill pool | 60–80 of 212 East Malaysia mills | MPOB processor directory + MSPO Trace register |
| Onboarding price | RM 450/household | Not yet tested against stated willingness to pay — our largest open question |
| Credit conversion rate | 8% of documented households | Requires a lender partnership that does not yet exist |

## **What Happens If We're Wrong**

| **Scenario** | **Assumptions Changed** | **Effect on the Plan** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Downside | Fee falls to RM 380, sign-ups 30% slower, costs 15% higher | Still cash-positive throughout; Break-even pushed to Year 3 instead of Year 2 |
| Upside | Fee rises to RM 500, two extra mills signed per year from Year 3 | Cumulative EBITDA roughly 40% higher by Year 5; break-even timing unaffected |

The variable that matters most is how fast we can sign up mills — not the exact price we charge. That is why Year 1's real job is producing two credible reference customers, not maximising revenue.

# **Appendix C — SWOT Summary & Research Protocol**

| **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
| --- | --- |
| Legal argument grounded directly in EUDR article text; correct payer identified (mill, not farmer); revenue recurrence is required by law, not by our sales effort | No prior palm oil industry experience on the founding team; revenue concentrated in one regulation through Year 3; onboarding price not yet tested by interview |
| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
| UK's own forest-risk regime arrives 2027, reusing the same evidence; rubber as a second commodity from Year 4; Malaysia's push for EU low-risk reclassification needs exactly the data we generate | A competitor such as Koltiva or TraceX enters this specific SME-mill segment; EUDR is delayed or weakened again; a CPO price collapse squeezes mill budgets |

## **Research Protocol**

Our primary research is semi-structured, about 12 minutes per interview, conducted with mill procurement and sustainability heads, MPOB-licensed dealers, and independent smallholders. Participants are sourced through the MPOB processor directory and the MSPO Trace register. We report our exact sample size, and we report answers that contradict our thesis alongside answers that support it — not just the ones that make the pitch look good.
